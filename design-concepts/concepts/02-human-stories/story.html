Amira, whose brother has been missing since 2023 (name changed)

Every Sunday, Amira sets one more cup on the table. Her brother was detained at a checkpoint in March 2023. Since then, she has written to four government offices, two prosecutors and one prison administration. None has confirmed where he is held, or whether he is alive.

Amira’s family is one of 64 we interviewed for this investigation. Their accounts follow a pattern: an arrest without a warrant, a transfer between facilities, and then silence.

A pattern of silence

Under international law, a disappearance continues for as long as the fate and whereabouts of the person remain unknown. Each unanswered letter is not only a failure of administration; it extends the harm to the whole family.

What families are asking for

The families’ requests are specific: a central register of everyone detained, the right to be notified within 48 hours of an arrest, and independent access to all places of detention.
