The Long Wait: Families of the Missing Are Still Searching for Answers

Over fourteen months, our researchers recorded the testimony of families whose relatives disappeared after detention. Their request to the authorities is simple: tell us where they are.

By Lena Ortiz, Senior Researcher·3 October 2026·12 min read

Share

Every Sunday, Amira sets one more cup on the table. Her brother was detained at a checkpoint in March 2023. Since then, she has written to four government offices, two prosecutors and one prison administration. None has confirmed where he is held, or whether he is alive.

Amira’s family is one of 64 we interviewed for this investigation. Their accounts follow a pattern: an arrest without a warrant, a transfer between facilities, and then silence. Officials told several families that their relatives had ‘never been registered’.

A pattern of silence

Under international law, a disappearance continues for as long as the fate and whereabouts of the person remain unknown. Each unanswered letter is not only a failure of administration; it extends the harm to the whole family.

I keep his chair at the table. One day someone will tell me where he is.

Amira, whose brother has been missing since 2023 (name changed)

We shared our findings with the relevant ministries in August 2026 and asked for a response to specific cases. At the time of publication, we had not received one.

What families are asking for

The families’ requests are specific: a central register of everyone detained, the right to be notified within 48 hours of an arrest, and independent access to all places of detention.
