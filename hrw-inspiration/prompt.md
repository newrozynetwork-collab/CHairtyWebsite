# Create 10 website design concepts in one client-ready PDF

Use the attached Human Rights Watch website reference pack as inspiration to create **10 distinct website design concepts for my client**, then deliver all 10 in **one polished PDF file**.

## Read the reference pack first

Read `REPORT.txt`, `AI-HANDOFF.txt`, `data/coverage.json`, and `data/screenshot-manifest.json`. Review the desktop, mobile, and Arabic screenshots, the overlapping screenshot slices, and `screenshots/design-measurements.json`. Use `REPORT.html` as a visual index if helpful.

The reference pack contains 500 successful HTML URL retrievals and selected page designs. It is not a complete archive or a screenshot of every page. Do not claim otherwise. Use the screenshots and measured data as evidence, and distinguish observations from your own proposed design choices.

Borrow useful principles: strong editorial hierarchy, documentary photography, readable long-form content, country/topic navigation, research publication layouts, impact storytelling, and clear participation actions. **Create original designs rather than copying HRW's branding or page layouts.** Use original or properly licensed copy, imagery, logos, and fonts.

## Establish the client brief

Before designing, ask me for the client's name, industry or mission, audience, logo/brand assets, required languages, main website goals, and required pages. Ask these in one concise message. If I ask you to proceed without that information, use a clearly labeled provisional brand and realistic sample content consistently across all 10 concepts. Never invent client facts or results.

## Make the 10 concepts meaningfully different

Each concept must have its own visual direction, page composition, typography, palette, imagery treatment, and navigation approach. Changing colors alone does not count as a different design. Keep the same client brief and core content across the concepts so the client can compare them fairly.

Consider these directions as starting points, adapting them to the brief:

1. **Editorial Authority:** a structured newspaper-style layout with a prominent lead story and dense, readable supporting coverage.
2. **Human Stories:** warm colors, intimate photography, and people-centered storytelling.
3. **Research & Evidence:** a precise publication-led design with strong report discovery and restrained visual decoration.
4. **Bold Advocacy:** expressive typography, decisive contrast, and prominent campaign actions.
5. **Quiet Minimalism:** spacious composition, selective imagery, and a calm reading experience.
6. **Global Atlas:** geographic discovery and region/country exploration as the main organizing idea.
7. **Contemporary Magazine:** varied editorial grids, distinctive art direction, and an engaging feature-story rhythm.
8. **Community & Participation:** welcoming design with clear opportunities to participate, attend, subscribe, or support.
9. **Institutional Trust:** an elegant organizational presence emphasizing credibility, transparency, and measurable impact.
10. **Digital Documentary:** cinematic photography and immersive investigations balanced with accessible reading layouts.

These are proposed creative directions, not claims about the original HRW website. You may substitute a stronger direction if it improves fit and keeps all 10 concepts distinct.

## Show actual designs

Produce polished visual website mockups, not just descriptions, mood boards, wireframes, or collections of existing HRW screenshots. Use realistic text lengths, consistent sample content, credible image crops, clear navigation, and visible calls to action. Show enough of each page to explain its structure and reading flow.

For **every concept**, include:

- A concept name and a short explanation of the design idea and its fit for the client.
- A large, legible **desktop homepage mockup**, including the header, hero, primary content modules, trust/impact section, and footer. Split a long page across PDF pages if necessary; do not shrink it until the text becomes unreadable.
- A **mobile homepage mockup** demonstrating an intentional responsive composition.
- One **interior-page mockup**, such as a story/article, report detail, topic hub, or service page, chosen to fit the client's site. Use the same interior-page type across concepts where practical for fair comparison.
- A compact style guide with palette hex codes, heading/body fonts, button treatment, and image direction.
- A short note explaining which reference principles informed the concept and what makes this concept different from the other nine.

If Arabic or another right-to-left language is required, design with real sample text, appropriate typography, and direction-aware navigation. Otherwise, do not add a language requirement without asking.

## Assemble one PDF for the client

Deliver **one PDF containing all 10 concepts**, suitable for sending directly to my client. Aim for approximately **35–45 pages**, but prioritize legibility and completeness over a rigid page count.

Suggested structure:

1. A clean cover with the client's name, the title “Website Design Concepts,” and the date.
2. A concise brief and contents page.
3. A consistent section for each of the 10 concepts, generally 3–4 pages per concept.
4. A final comparison table showing visual character, content emphasis, best fit, and key tradeoffs.
5. A recommendation of 2–3 concepts with specific reasons tied to the brief, plus a simple client selection form or decision page.

Use consistent PDF typography, margins, page numbers, concept labels, and image framing. Make the report easy to browse and compare. Keep wording clear and client-facing; omit internal AI instructions, tool logs, implementation jargon, and unfinished placeholders. Clearly label the mockups as **design concepts**, not functioning websites.

Do not turn the original HRW screenshot gallery into the client presentation. The PDF should showcase your newly created concepts. If you include reference material, keep it minimal, label and attribute it clearly, and place it separately from the proposed designs.

## Verify and deliver

Render and inspect every PDF page before delivery. Fix clipping, overlap, tiny text, blurry mockups, inconsistent alignment, missing images, and awkward page breaks. Confirm that the file contains exactly 10 distinct concepts and that every concept has desktop, mobile, interior-page, and style-guide material.

Provide a downloadable PDF named `Client_Website_10_Design_Concepts.pdf` (or replace “Client” with the client's name). Also provide the editable mockups or source files when supported, but keep the main client deliverable as **one PDF**.

If you cannot generate or attach a PDF in your environment, state that limitation before starting and propose a concrete exportable alternative. Do not claim a PDF exists unless you have actually created and verified it.
